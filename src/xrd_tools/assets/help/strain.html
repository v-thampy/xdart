How residual stress tilts a sin²ψ line, and how Strain Analysis reads it

Stress in a film stretches some lattice planes and squeezes others, depending on how they are tilted. Measuring one reflection's plane spacing at several tilts ψ turns that into a straight line whose slope is the stress. This guide starts with that physics, then shows how each number Strain Analysis reports follows from it.

The physics

Strain is a change of plane spacing

A reflection measures the spacing d of one family of lattice planes. Under stress that spacing changes, and the strain is the fractional change from the stress-free spacing d₀: ε = (d − d₀)/d₀. In-plane tension pulls apart the planes that stand upright in the film, while the Poisson effect pulls together the planes that lie flat, so the strain a reflection shows depends on how its planes are tilted. That tilt is the handle on the stress.

Other stress states

Uniaxial or unequal biaxial stress makes the slope depend on the in-plane direction of the measurement, and shear in the plane of the surface normal splits the line into two branches at +ψ and −ψ for one in-plane direction. Both need each point's in-plane direction. A grazing-incidence fiber map gives only the polar angle, and its ±χ branches are mirrors, not directions, so Strain Analysis fits equibiaxial stress and refuses the others by name until a geometry model gives each point its direction.

How Strain Analysis fits

From a map to points

  • The q–χ map is cut into χ sectors, given by their centres and width, on one or both branches. A sector with too little finite data, or one you leave out (named by its signed χ centre), is excluded.
  • Each sector's reflection is fitted with a pseudo-Voigt, Pearson VII or split pseudo-Voigt profile for its q, and so its d = 2π/q and the error of d.
  • The points enter one regression of d against sin²ψ over both branches. Each branch also gets its own descriptive fit.

The regression and its uncertainty

Choosing d₀

  • Typed: a spacing you know, with its error if you have one.
  • Phase library: a CIF or lattice phase's line at the record's wavelength. It is a chosen reference, not a measured stress-free value, so the absolute stress inherits its error.
  • Stress-free reference: the weighted mean spacing of a stress-free sample's result.
  • ψ*: the fitted line's d at the strain-free tilt. It needs the elastic constants and is model-derived, not independent of the fit.

Refraction at grazing incidence

Close to the critical angle, refraction at the surface shifts the measured q, and so the slope. Strain Analysis estimates how much refraction alone would move the slope, for the fit's own points, through the shared grazing-incidence correction owner. It reports the stress as apparent when the incidence or any exit angle is within three critical angles, when the shift is at least a tenth of the measured slope, or when no material is chosen. It applies no correction: that is a later correction step.
